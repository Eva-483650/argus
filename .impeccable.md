## Design Context

### Users
Primary users are competition judges reviewing the Argus project in a desktop browser setting. They need to understand the research value quickly, see a convincing demo, and form confidence in the team's technical maturity without digging through dense academic material.

### Brand Personality
Argus should feel frontier-grade, productized, and trustworthy. The desired tone is advanced, polished, and calm. The interface should communicate that this is not just a lab prototype, but a disciplined research outcome with strong engineering execution.

### Aesthetic Direction
The visual direction should combine restrained cinematic storytelling with high-end product presentation. Positive references include Apple's Vision Pro presentation pages, Firewatch-style scroll reveal, WebGI-style dramatic 3D entrances and scroll-driven narrative, and Aurochs-style clean backgrounds with controlled accent usage. The site should avoid marketplace aesthetics, generic admin dashboards, cyberpunk neon, blue-purple gradient template styling, stacked-card layouts, and emoji-led UI decoration. Both dark and light themes should be supported, with the same design language preserved across both.

### Design Principles
1. Lead with research value: every major section should help judges understand the innovation, not just admire the visuals.
2. Treat the site like a product launch, not a document dump: use scroll-driven sequencing, deliberate reveals, and strong narrative pacing.
3. Keep the atmosphere calm and premium: rely on contrast, composition, and motion discipline rather than loud gradients or excessive effects.
4. Make technical credibility visible: demo moments, terminology, metrics, and interaction polish should reinforce trust and seriousness.
5. Design for desktop presentation quality: prioritize large-screen composition, smooth scroll choreography, and high-finish visual refinement suitable for competition and defense settings.
