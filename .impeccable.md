## Design Context

### Users
Primary users are competition judges and defense reviewers viewing the Argus project on desktop screens during formal presentations, reviews, and scripted demonstrations. They are not using a self-service testing product. They need to understand scenario selection, multimodal inputs, inference results, and GT comparison immediately, with minimal reading burden.

### Brand Personality
Argus should feel disciplined, credible, and refined. The tone should be professional, calm, and presentation-ready. The interface should communicate that this is a finished research demonstration with strong engineering execution, not a temporary lab tool, academic dashboard, or upload-based online demo.

### Aesthetic Direction
Keep the existing Argus language: restrained cinematic atmosphere, serif-led academic polish, warm gold accents, and a premium demonstration-console frame across both dark and light themes. For the Project page specifically, the correct structure is a left-narrow and right-wide composition. The left side behaves like a scene selector. The right side is the dominant presentation stage, containing explicit segmented controls, a large 640x512 image viewport, and a compact speaker-note style explanation area. Avoid three-column parity, long explanatory prose, weak pseudo-buttons, stacked cards, admin patterns, cyberpunk neon, and document-like layouts.

### Design Principles
1. Lead with the defense interaction: the first glance must reveal where to click and what changes when clicked.
2. Make the stage dominant: scenario switching and image viewing are the core actions, and the image viewport should own the page.
3. Use explicit controls: scene items, view switches, and modality switches must read as buttons immediately.
4. Compress the copy: supporting notes should feel like cue cards, not report paragraphs.
5. Fit the real media: the layout must be designed around the 640x512 image ratio rather than forcing the image into a balanced three-column template.
6. Preserve the existing Argus system: inherit the site's palette, typography, frame, and calm premium tone rather than inventing a disconnected visual language.
